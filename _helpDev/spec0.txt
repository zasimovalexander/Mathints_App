1. GCD and LCM COMPUTATION
mu0
{rules}
● A PRIME natural number is divisible only by 1 and itself, while a COMPOSITE number has more than two divisors. The number one is an exception – it has only one divisor.||● Divisibility rules: if the sum of the digits of a number is divisible by 3 → the whole number is divisible by 3, and if the last digit of the number:|  • is divisible by 2 → the whole number is divisible by 2|  • is 0 → the whole number is divisible by 5, 10|  • is 5 → the whole number is divisible by 5.||● If a natural number is factored into prime factors, then only a single unique combination exists.||● To find the Greatest Common DIVISOR (GCD) or the Least Common MULTIPLE (LCM) of several natural numbers, factor these numbers into primes, and then calculate the product:|  • for GCD → only the common factors, taking each one once with its smallest occurring exponent|  • for LCM → all factors, and among the common ones take the factor with the largest exponent.|
{ui}
● Numbers input:|  • using ";" you can specify up to {qty} numbers|  • the digit length of a single number cannot exceed {rng}|  • the number 1 is ignored, as well as repeated numbers.||● Result output:|  • for convenience, the "_" symbol groups digit blocks during factorization|  • in the "artfs" folder, a file is stored with the last solution: "Factoring.txt".
2. COMMON FRACTIONS
mu1
{rules}
● A number that can be written in the form P/Q, where the NUMERATOR P and the DENOMINATOR Q are natural numbers, is called a RATIONAL number or simply a FRACTION. A fraction with numerator N and denominator 1 is another form of writing a natural number: N/1 = N. For fractions, the commutative and associative laws of addition and multiplication hold, just as for natural numbers, and numerical expressions follow the same rules of order of operations.||● If the numerator and denominator of a fraction are multiplied by the same natural number, the resulting fraction is equal to the original one: P/Q = [P·N]/[Q·N] – the FUNDAMENTAL property of a fraction (you can obtain fractions that represent the same number written in different ways). Conversely, if the numerator and denominator of a fraction have a common factor, the fraction can be reduced by that factor, i.e., divide numerator and denominator by it – the result will be an equal fraction: [P·N]/[Q·N] = P/Q. A fraction is called IRREDUCIBLE if its numerator and denominator have no common prime factors. For every fraction, there exists exactly one equivalent irreducible, obtained by dividing numerator and denominator by their GCD in the original fraction. If the numerator of a fraction is divisible by the denominator with no remainder, then the fraction equals the quotient of this division.||● If several fractions have different denominators, they can be brought to a COMMON denominator, which is the LCM of these denominators. Then each fraction gains its own ADDITIONAL multiplier (usually written above the numerator to the left), by which the numerator must also be multiplied.||● Of two fractions with the same denominator, the greater one is the one with the larger numerator: P/Q > R/Q, P > R. To compare two fractions with different denominators, they must be brought to a common denominator, and then the previous rule is applied. If in a fraction the numerator < denominator, it is PROPER (fraction < 1), and if the numerator ≥ denominator – IMPROPER (fraction ≥ 1). An improper fraction can be represented as a MIXED number by dividing the numerator by the denominator with a remainder: the quotient becomes the whole part, the remainder becomes the numerator, and the denominator remains the same.||● The sum (diff) of fractions with a common denominator is a fraction whose numerator equals the sum (diff) of the numerators, and the denominator is the same as the given fractions: P/Q±R/Q = [P±R]/Q. To add (subtract) two fractions with different denominators: P/F±R/K, they must be brought to a common denominator: F·A = K·B = Q, and then the rule for adding (subtracting) fractions with a common denominator applies: P/F±R/K = [P·A±R·B]/Q (or this way: P/F±R/K = [P·K±R·F]/[F·K]). The number 0 is considered equal to a fraction of the form 0/Q (Q≠0): P/Q-P/Q = [P-P]/Q = 0.||● The product of two fractions is a fraction whose numerator equals the product of their numerators, and whose denominator equals the product of their denominators: P/F·R/K = [P·R]/[F·K]. When multiplying a natural number N by a fraction, you may multiply only the numerator of the fraction: N·P/Q = [N·P]/Q (N = N/1). Fractions (numbers) of the form P/Q and Q/P (P≠0, Q≠0) are called RECIPROCALS: P/Q·Q/P = 1.||● To divide one fraction by another, multiply the dividend by the reciprocal of the divisor: P/F:R/K = P/F·K/R = [P·K]/[F·R]. The quotient of any two natural numbers equals the fraction whose numerator is the dividend and denominator is the divisor (the fraction bar represents the division sign; when it appears multiple times, the equals sign is placed at the bar that represents the last division in order). To divide a fraction by a natural number N, multiply its denominator by that number: P/Q:N = P/[Q·N].
{ui}
● Numbers input:|  • numerators and denominators are separated with ";", just like the fractions themselves: n1;d1;n2;d2, i.e., there should be {qty} numbers in total|  • the digit length of each fraction part cannot exceed {rng}|  • the denominator cannot be equal to 0, which is also ignored for the numerator.||● Result output:|  • the first block "Comparison" contains for each fraction: mixed* (if it exists), fraction, reduction*, bringing to a common denominator|  • the blocks "Addition" and "Subtraction" (the smaller is subtracted from the larger) include: expression, common fraction (the values previously brought to a common denominator), reduction*, mixed*|  • the blocks "Multiplication" and "Division" contain: expression, common fraction (the values previously reduced), cross reduction*, mixed*|  • when reducing fractions, the numbers are represented as multiplying their remainders by the GCD.||● "Rules" tab:|  • the symbols "[]" indicate that the entire expression belongs to the numerator (denominator)|  • the "/" sign is equivalent to the horizontal bar in the standard representation of common fractions.
